General information

Golden-Aster ( Heterotheca species), particularly Heterotheca villosa (Hairy Golden Aster) and related species like Heterotheca inuloides (Mexican Arnica), has been traditionally used by various Indigenous groups across North America for medicinal purposes. [2] The traditional use of Golden-Aster by Indigenous peoples demonstrates sophisticated understanding of plant medicine, with scientific research now validating many of these traditional applications through identification of specific bioactive compounds and their mechanisms of action. [5]

Traditional Indigenous Uses

Its leaves and aerial parts were gathered and made into poultices or teas to ease the pain in joints and bones. Those who suffered from arthritis or rheumatism found comfort in its healing touch. [1]

For wounds and skin troubles, the fresh leaves were pressed gently against cuts, bruises, or irritated skin. They cooled and soothed, helping the body to close and cleanse itself. The same plant offered medicine for the lungs and breath. When coughs or bronchitis took hold, its leaves and flowers were boiled into a tea or used for steam inhalation to clear the chest. [6] When the stomach was troubled, the roots and upper parts of the plant were brewed into a mild decoction to ease pain and help with digestion, bringing balance back to the body. [2]

Whole plants were used in washes for eczema, insect bites, and other irritations, while oils drawn from its essence were used to cleanse wounds and prevent infection. [5] Sometimes the leaves and stems were infused into oil and rubbed on sore muscles or temples to relieve pain and headaches. [7]

Biochemical Basis for Medicinal Properties

Key Bioactive Compounds

Golden-Aster contains several classes of bioactive compounds responsible for its medicinal properties: [5]

  1. Sesquiterpene Lactones

Structure and Function:

  • Primary compounds: 7-hydroxyfrullanolide, inulin, and various germacranolide derivatives [5]
  • Chemical Structure: These are 15-carbon compounds with a characteristic lactone ring
  • Mechanism: Anti-inflammatory action through inhibition of NF-κB pathway and cyclooxygenase enzymes [5]
  1. Flavonoids

Key Compounds:

  • Quercetin, kaempferol, and their glycosides [5]
  • Apigenin and luteolin derivatives
  • Antioxidant Activity: Free radical scavenging through electron donation [5]
  • Chemical Reaction : R-OH + R'• → R-O• + R'H (radical scavenging)
  1. Essential Oils

Volatile Compounds:

  • Monoterpenes: α-pinene, β-pinene, limonene [5]
  • Sesquiterpenes: β-caryophyllene, germacrene D [5]
  • Mechanism: Antimicrobial activity through membrane disruption
  1. Phenolic Compounds
  • Caffeic acid, chlorogenic acid, and ferulic acid derivatives [5]
  • Function: Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties through enzyme inhibition

Biochemical Mechanisms

Anti-inflammatory Pathway:

Tissue Injury → Arachidonic Acid Release → COX-1/COX-2 Activation ↓ (Inhibited by sesquiterpene lactones) Prostaglandin/Leukotriene Production → Inflammation [5]

Antioxidant Mechanism:

ROS Formation → Cellular Damage ↓ (Neutralized by flavonoids) [5]

Flavonoid + ROS → Stable Quinone + H₂O

Wound Healing Process:

  1. Hemostasis: Tannins promote blood clotting [4]
  2. Inflammation Control: Sesquiterpenes reduce inflammatory response [5]
  3. Proliferation: Growth factors enhanced by plant compounds
  4. Remodeling: Antioxidants protect new tissue formation

Chemical Structures of Key Biomolecules

Quercetin (Major Flavonoid)

  • Molecular Formula: C₁₅H₁₀O₇

Fig 35.5: Chemical structure diagram of quercetin (3,3′,4′,5,7-pentahydroxyflavone; molecular formula C₁₅H₁₀O₇), the principal flavonoid antioxidant identified in Heterotheca species, showing its tricyclic benzopyrone scaffold with five hydroxyl groups at positions 3, 5, 7, 3′, and 4′ that confer potent free-radical scavenging and anti-inflammatory activity.

  • Structure: Pentahydroxyflavone with hydroxyl groups at positions 3, 5, 7, 3′, and 4′ [5]
  • Function: Potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory agent [5]

7-Hydroxyfrullanolide (Sesquiterpene Lactone)

  • Molecular Formula: C₁₅H₂₀O₄
  • Structure: Germacranolide skeleton with hydroxyl and lactone functional groups [5]
  • Function: Primary anti-inflammatory compound [5]

β-Caryophyllene (Essential Oil Component)

Fig 35.6: Chemical structure diagram of β-caryophyllene (molecular formula C₁₅H₂₄), a bicyclic sesquiterpene and major volatile component of Heterotheca essential oils. The structure highlights its unique cyclobutane ring fused to a nine-membered ring, and its endocyclic double bonds — features that enable selective binding to the cannabinoid CB2 receptor and underpin its documented anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties.

  • Molecular Formula: C₁₅H₂₄
  • Structure: Bicyclic sesquiterpene with CB2 receptor binding capability [3]
  • Function: Anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties [3]
