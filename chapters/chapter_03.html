3 Soapberry

Names

Common name –  Soapberry

Scientific Name : Shepherdia canadensis

Other Names : Xúsum, Canadian Buffaloberry, Russet Buffaloberry, Rabbitberry, Soopolallie (Soapberry in Chinook), Foamberry

Fig 3.1: Clusters of bright red, translucent soapberries (Shepherdia canadensis), notable for their use in traditional Indigenous remedies and foods.

Image Source: https://plants.hwulmuhwqun.ca/plant/sxwesum-fruit-soapberry/

General Information

Soapberry is a deciduous tree growing 25-50 feet tall with pinnately compound leaves (8-18 leaflets per leaf). The tree produces clusters of small white flowers followed by round, translucent golden-yellow berries (drupes) about 1/2-3/4 inch diameter. The berries persist on the tree through winter. Each fruit contains a single hard, black, round seed surrounded by translucent pulp. When the pulp is mixed with water, it creates a soapy lather due to high saponin content, hence the common name.

IMPORTANT: The saponin-rich pulp is toxic if ingested in quantity and should not be taken internally except in very diluted, controlled preparations. [6]

Chemical Content: The fruit pulp contains 10-38% saponins (triterpene glycosides), making it one of the richest natural sources of saponins. [14] The seeds contain oil (40-50%) rich in fatty acids. [14]

Traditional Indigenous Uses

When mixed with water, the berries made a rich foam that the people used to wash their hair and skin, keeping both clean and healthy. [3],[11] This lather soothed rashes, dandruff, and irritations of the scalp, and helped to drive away lice. [3],[11] The same soapy wash was used to cool fevers and ease the heat of aching joints or swollen limbs. [3],[11] A poultice made from the crushed fruit could cleanse wounds and help them heal. [3],[11]

Beyond its physical uses, soapberry was a part of ceremony and community life. [3],[18] The people used its frothy lather in purification baths before gatherings, believing it could wash away bad energy and bring renewal to the spirit. [3] The smooth, hard seeds were polished and strung into necklaces, earrings, and buttons. [11],[18] Though the plant was powerful, it holds compounds that can be harmful if misused. In the right hands, however, it offered cleansing for the body, beauty for the hair, and purification for the soul.

CRITICAL SAFETY WARNINGS

  • Fruit pulp is TOXIC if swallowed in significant amounts [6],[12]
  • Causes gastroenteritis, vomiting, diarrhea [6]
  • Eye irritation if soap gets in eyes [17]
  • Seeds are non-toxic but indigestible [14]
  • External use only unless under expert guidance with extreme dilution [6]

One of the most well-known uses of soapberries is in a dessert once referred to as “Indian Ice Cream”. This is made by vigorously whisking the berries in water which produces a stiff foam which is then eaten, sometimes with the addition of sugar or sweeteners. [3],[11]

Soapberries were also eaten to treat diabetes and heart conditions. [2],[11] They were also though to help ward off insects such as mosquitos. [11] The branches of the soapberry plant were also used to create yellow dyes. [11]

The berry of the soapberry plant contains several chemical components resulting in its unique properties. Saponins the general structure of which is shown in Figure 3 consist of a polycyclic aglycone and a sugar side chain. [19] As a result, saponins are amphipathic which causes them to foam when mixed vigorously with water. [7] Saponins can, however, cause gastrointestinal irritation, particularly when consumed in large quantities. [6]

Fig 3.3: The general chemical structure of a saponin molecule, showcasing its amphipathic nature with a polycyclic aglycone core and a sugar side chain. [19]

Another important constituent of soapberries is dietary fiber which includes polysaccharides like pectin and cellulose. [5] Pectin in particular is used in making jams and preserves. Soapberries also contain other bioactive ingredients, such as a large number of polyphenols and carotenoids such as lycopene and apo-6’ lycopenoate. [13]

Chemical Reactions and Biochemical Mechanisms

Surfactant/Detergent Properties

Micelle Formation

The Amphipathic Nature of Saponins: [19]

In water, saponin molecules orient with:

– Hydrophobic triterpene cores facing inward (away from water)

– Hydrophilic sugar chains facing outward (toward water)

Result: Micelle formation (spherical aggregates) [7]

Cleaning Action

Saponin micelles + Oils/dirt (lipophilic) → Oils trapped in micelle core → Micelle-oil complex soluble in water → Dirt/oil washed away [14]

This is how soapberry works as soap!

Antimicrobial Mechanisms

Membrane Disruption

Saponins + Bacterial cell membrane → Insertion of lipophilic triterpene into lipid bilayer → Membrane destabilization → Pore formation → Leakage of cellular contents → Cell lysis and death [15]

Particularly effective against Gram-positive bacteria [15]

Cholesterol Interaction (Mammalian vs Bacterial Cells): [1]

Saponins have high affinity for sterols:

In bacterial membranes (no cholesterol):

– Saponins disrupt membrane directly

– High antimicrobial activity

In mammalian cell membranes (with cholesterol):

– Saponins bind cholesterol [1]

– Can cause toxicity (why internal use is dangerous)

– Selective toxicity basis

Fungal Activity

Saponins + Fungal ergosterol (membrane sterol) → Complex formation → Membrane disruption → Antifungal activity [15]

Antiparasitic Effects

Helminth (Worm) Toxicity

Saponins → Damage to worm tegument (outer covering) → Paralysis of worms → Death or expulsion [4]

Traditional use as fish poison demonstrates toxicity to lower organisms [8]

Spermicidal/Contraceptive Action

Sperm Membrane Disruption

Saponins + Sperm cell membrane → Membrane permeabilization → Loss of motility → Cell death [10],[17]

Anti-Inflammatory Effects

Aglycone Anti-Inflammatory Activity

Hederagenin and oleanolic acid (aglycones) → Inhibit NF-κB pathway → Reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) → Anti-inflammatory effect [10],[15]

Also: COX-2 inhibition → Reduced prostaglandin synthesis [15]

Wound Healing Support

Antimicrobial Protection

Saponin-containing poultice → Prevents wound infection → Cleaner healing environment [9],[11]

Mild Irritant Effect

Low-concentration saponins → Mild irritation → Increased local blood flow → Enhanced healing (rubefacient effect) [9]

Hair and Scalp Benefits

Cleansing and Degreasing

Saponins remove excess sebum (oil) from scalp: [17]

Sebum (lipophilic) + Saponin micelles → Oil solubilized and washed away → Clean scalp, reduced dandruff [17]

Lice Treatment

Saponins + Lice exoskeleton (waxy cuticle) → Disruption of protective layer → Dehydration of lice → Death [16]
