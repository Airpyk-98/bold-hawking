Names

Common name – Canada Goldenrod

Scientific name – Solidago canadensis

General information

Canada Goldenrod, a native, long-lived perennial forms large, dense patches [7] . Canada Goldenrod is named in honor of the many medicinal uses indigenous tribes found for the plant [5] . Its genus name “Solidago,” originates from the Latin word “solidare” which means “to make whole.” [8] The flowers and roots were widely used. Canadian goldenrod has been used in folk medicine for centuries as urological and antiphlogistical therapy, febrifuge, analgesic, gastro-intestinal tract and liver aids, and in burns and ulcer treatment [3] . Indigenous peoples used Solidago canadensis medicinally for treating colds, fevers, and inflammation [7] . Some preparations were made as teas for respiratory ailments, while poultices were used for wounds and skin conditions [7] .

Traditional Indigenous Uses

Its golden flowers and leaves were gathered and brewed into tea to cleanse the kidneys and bladder, helping to flush away infection and stones [5] . The same tea eased colds, coughs, and breathing troubles, and was said to calm the chest for those suffering from asthma or tuberculosis [11] . When wounds, burns, or sores appeared, poultices and washes made from the leaves and flowers helped draw out infection and close the skin, as the plant was known to be both astringent and healing [10] .

Goldenrod also offered relief for pain and inflammation. The people used its aerial parts as tea or in external applications to ease arthritis, rheumatism, and swelling [12] . The same preparation soothed stomach problems supported the liver and brought strength to the digestive system [7] . Some used it to help regulate the heart and blood pressure, while others found it calmed menstrual discomfort and aided women’s health during menopause [7] . Teas made from its flowers were drunk to reduce fever and pain, and mouth rinses from its leaves helped with sore throats and mouth infections [8] .

Biochemical Basis for Medicinal Properties

Major Bioactive Compound Classes

  1. Flavonoids (Primary Active Compounds) – S. canadensis is rich in specialized metabolite flavonoids (quercetin, kaempferol, and their glycosides, astragalin, and rutoside) [3]

Key Flavonoid Compounds:

  • Rutin (Quercetin-3-O-rutinoside): The largest share of the total pool of flavonoid glycosides in the leaves of the studied plants belonged to quercetin-3-O-beta-rutinoside (rutin) [3]
  • Isoquercitrin (Quercetin-3-O-β-glucoside) [3]
  • Astragalin (Kaempferol-3-O-β-glucoside) [3]
  • Hyperoside (Quercetin-3-O-β-galactoside) [3]

Chemical Structure and Activity:

Molecular mechanisms:

– Antioxidant activity through hydrogen donation [3]

– Anti-inflammatory via NF-κB pathway inhibition [2]

– Capillary stabilization through collagen cross-linking [3]

  1. Triterpenoid Saponins

Four new bisdesmosidic saponins each containing eight carbohydrate units were isolated from Solidago canadensis, identified as bayogeninglycosides (canadensissaponins 1-4) [9] .

  • Canadensissaponin 1-4: Complex bisdesmosidic structures [9]
  • Anti-fungal activity: The plant contains saponins that are antifungal and act specifically against the Candida fungus which is the cause of vaginal and oral thrush [9]
  • Immunomodulatory effects: Polysaccharide complexes with immune-stimulating properties [9]
  1. Phenolic Acids

Phenolic acids present include caffeic acid, chlorogenic acid, ferulic acid, synaptic, and vanillin acids [3] .

Major Phenolic Compounds:

  • Chlorogenic acid (3-O-caffeoylquinic acid): in the water extract, it is chlorogenic acid (834.50 ± 9.75 mg/g extract) [1]
  • Neochlorogenic acid (5-O-caffeoylquinic acid) [1]
  • Caffeic acid derivatives [1]
  1. Essential Oil Components

Essential oils include cadinene, α and β pinene, myrcene, limonene, sabinene, and germacren D [1]

Terpenoid Profile:

  • α-Pinene: Bronchodilator, antimicrobial [1]
  • β-Pinene: Anti-inflammatory [1]
  • Germacrene D: Antimicrobial, insecticidal [1]
  • δ-Cadinene: Antimicrobial properties [1]
  1. Diterpenes

A new labdane diterpene, 9α,16ξ-dihydroxy-6-oxo-7,13-labdadien-15,16-olide (solicanolide, 1) was isolated from S. canadensis flowers [4] .

Pharmacological Mechanisms

Diuretic Activity (Primary Mechanism)

The ethanol extract and its ethyl acetate fraction (EA) showed the highest aquaretic activities (91 and 58% at a dose of 400 mg/Kg b.wt., respectively) compared to 100% of furosemide at 20 mg/Kg b.wt. [1]

Mechanism of Action:

  1. Increased glomerular filtration rate [1]
  2. Enhanced sodium and chloride excretion [1]
  3. Vasodilation of renal blood vessels [1]
  4. Inhibition of sodium reabsorption in distal tubules [1]

Anti-inflammatory Pathways

Its main chemical constituents are flavonoids (mainly derived from quercetin and kaempferol), C6-C1 and C6-C3 compounds, terpenes (mostly from the essential oil), and a large number of saponin molecules [3]

Molecular Mechanisms:

  • COX-2 inhibition by flavonoids [2]
  • Lipoxygenase pathway suppression [2]
  • NF-κB transcription factor inhibition [2]
  • Cytokine production reduction [2]

Antimicrobial Activity

  1. canadensis and S. gigantea showed greater antibacterial over S. virgaurea for gram-positive bacteria [6]

Antimicrobial Mechanisms:

  • Cell membrane disruption by saponins [9]
  • Protein synthesis inhibition [6]
  • DNA replication interference [6]
  • Biofilm formation prevention [6]

Chemical Reactions and Molecular Interactions

Flavonoid Antioxidant Mechanism

Quercetin + Free Radical• → Quercetin Radical• + H•

(Stable resonance structure prevents chain propagation) [3]

Rutin + DPPH• → Rutin-DPPH Complex (stable, colored)

(DPPH radical scavenging assay principle) [3]

Saponin-Membrane Interaction

Saponin + Cell Membrane Cholesterol → Saponin-Cholesterol Complex

(Membrane destabilization leading to cell lysis) [9]

Canadensissaponin + Candida Cell Wall → Pore Formation

(Antifungal mechanism through membrane permeabilization) [9]

Diuretic Compound Activity

A strong correlation existed between the total phenolic and flavonoid contents and the investigated aquaretic activity of the different extracts and fractions [1]

Phenolic Compounds + Renal Transporters → Modified Ion Transport [1]

Flavonoids + Aquaporin Channels → Enhanced Water Excretion [1]

Modern Scientific Validation

Quantitative Analysis

EA showed the highest total phenolic and flavonoid contents among the tested fractions of the ethanol and aqueous extracts (9.38 ± 0.004 g GAE and 39.75 ± 0.005 g RE/100 g dried extract, respectively) [1]

Bioactive Compound Isolation

Eight flavonoids, 2 phenolic acids and 1 nucleoside were isolated from EA. This is the first report of a comparative study between the aquaretic activities of the different extracts, fractions and essential oil of S. canadensis, as well as isolation of thyimidine (1), isorhamnetin-3-O-β-ᴅ-glucopyranoside (2), kaempferol-3-O-(6″-O-acetyl)-β-ᴅ-glucopyranoside (4), quercetin-3-O-(6″-O-acetyl)-β-ᴅ-glucopyranoside (5), and kaempferol-3-O-β-ᴅ-apiofuranoside (7) from genus Solidago [1]

Therapeutic Applications

It does seem to act like a diuretic, and is used in Europe to treat urinary tract inflammation and to prevent or treat kidney stones. In fact, goldenrod is often found in teas to help “flush out” kidney stones and stop inflammatory diseases of the urinary tract [8]

Safety and Traditional Preparation

Traditional Safety Profile

Safety in pregnancy is unknown [8]

Optimal Extraction Methods

The maximum free radical scavenging activity was greatest in a standard infusion. In other words, drink your tea! [3]

Ethanol (70%) was the best solvent for extracting phenolic compounds from Solidago canadensis L., while water was the best solvent for the extraction of its saponins [1]
