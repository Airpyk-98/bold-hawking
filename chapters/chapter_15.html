Lavender Salve

Fig 15.1: Fresh Lavender Salve, prepared to soothe skin irritations, ease muscles, and provide calming relief.

General Information

True lavender ( Lavandula spp.) is NOT native to North America [7, 11] . Desert lavender, a flowering shrub with some physical similarities to lavender that actually is completely unrelated to lavender is what is used here [4] .

Two Different Plants:

  1. TRUE LAVENDER ( Lavandula angustifolia , L. latifolia , L. x intermedia )
  1. DESERT LAVENDER ( Hyptis emoryi , syn. Condea emoryi )

DESERT LAVENDER ( Hyptis emoryi )

Plant Description

Physical Characteristics: Desert Lavender is an evergreen shrub 3-9 feet tall with aromatic gray-green leaves, silvery appearance from dense hairs, and tubular lavender-blue flowers that bloom January through May. The plant releases a strong fragrance similar to true lavender when leaves are crushed, hence the common name [7] .

Traditional Indigenous Uses – Desert Lavender

Its gentle scent calmed the mind and body, easing worries, sleeplessness, and the restlessness that sometimes takes hold of the heart [4, 8, 9] . The leaves and flowers, steeped into a warm tea or breathed in as steam, were used to quiet anxiety and bring rest to those troubled by dreams or grief [1, 5, 11, 16] . Lavender was also called upon to ease coughs, colds, and congestion, helping the breath to flow freely again [1, 11] . When fever rose in the body, its tea helped to cool and balance the spirit [6, 11] .

For stomach troubles, such as cramps or indigestion, the tea brought comfort [10, 11] , while poultices made from its crushed leaves were laid on wounds, burns, or insect bites to draw out infection and soothe the skin [1, 3, 12] . Lavender’s gentle oil eased sore muscles and headaches [13, 14] , and women drank its tea to find relief from menstrual cramps or to aid recovery after childbirth [11] . In times of purification, lavender branches were placed in bath water, cleansing not only the body but also the spirit [4, 11] . Its fragrance, carried on the wind or tucked into clothing, brought freshness to the home and kept away biting insects [2, 11] .

Preparation and Safety Considerations

Desert Lavender Preparations:

  • Infusion methods [11]
  • Fresh or dried leaves can be used [11]
  • Traditional poultices made from crushed fresh leaves [1]

Safety Notes:

  • Generally very safe for topical use [2, 17]
  • Rare allergic reactions possible (patch test first) [15, 17]
  • Safe during pregnancy (topical use) [17]
  • Can cause contact dermatitis in sensitive individuals [15, 17]
  • Essential oil should be diluted (not used neat) except for spot treatment of bites/burns [17]
  • Internal use of essential oil not recommended (different from tea) [17]
