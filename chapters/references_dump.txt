LI: <li>   Lactone Ring (Sesquiterpene Lactones): Electrophilic sites enable protein alkylation; anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial  </li>
LI: <li>   Multiple Hydroxyl Groups (Flavonoids, Tannins): Hydrogen bonding with proteins; antioxidant electron donation; astringent effects  </li>
LI: <li>   Polyphenolic Structure: Protein precipitation capability; antimicrobial activity through membrane disruption  </li>
LI: <li>   Catechol Groups: Strong metal chelation; enhanced antioxidant activity; enzyme inhibition  </li>
LI: <li>   Mucilage Polysaccharides: Extensive hydroxyl groups create viscous hydrogels; physical protection of tissues  </li>
LI: <li>   Lipophilic Terpenoids: Membrane penetration; antimicrobial membrane disruption  </li>
P: <p><strong>   Traditional Preparation:  </strong></p>
LI: <li>   Tea: 1-2 teaspoons dried aerial parts per cup; steep 10-15 minutes  </li>
LI: <li>   Poultice: Crush fresh leaves or moisten dried leaves; apply directly to affected area  </li>
LI: <li>   Chest Compress: Strong decoction applied as warm compress  </li>
LI: <li>   Smoking Mixture: Dried leaves (some species); ceremonial or medicinal use  </li>
P: <p><strong>   Safety Notes:  </strong></p>
LI: <li>   Generally considered safe based on traditional use  </li>
LI: <li>   Sesquiterpene lactones (if present) may cause allergic reactions in sensitive individuals (Asteraceae family allergy)  </li>
LI: <li>   Avoid if allergic to ragweed, chrysanthemums, marigolds, or other Asteraceae  </li>
LI: <li>   Pregnancy/breastfeeding: Insufficient safety data; use with caution  </li>
LI: <li>   No significant drug interactions documented  </li>
LI: <li>   External use generally safer than internal  </li>
